You’re hitting on the core issue - **the interface needs to match how dealership staff actually think and work**, not follow generic web app patterns.

## Ditch the Sidebar for Task-Based Navigation

**Top navigation with workflow tabs** - Organize around actual dealer processes: “Inventory,” “Sales Process,” “Service,” “Customer Management,” “Reports.” Each tab reveals contextual sub-navigation.

**Dashboard-first approach** - Land users on a role-specific dashboard (Sales Manager vs Service Advisor vs Finance) with quick actions and today’s priorities front and center.

**Contextual navigation** - When viewing a customer, show related actions right there: “Schedule Service,” “View Purchase History,” “Process Trade-in” rather than making them navigate elsewhere.

## Route Structure That Matches Mental Models

Instead of `/admin/vehicles/123/edit`, use paths like:

- `/inventory/lot-management`
- `/customer/john-smith/service-history`
- `/deals/pending/review`
- `/service/appointments/today`

**Breadcrumbs that make sense** - “Customer: John Smith > Current Deal > Trade Appraisal” not “Users > ID:12345 > Transactions > Form”

## Workflow-Centered Design

**Task completion flows** - Guide users through complete processes like “New Customer Sale” or “Service Write-up” with clear progress indicators and next steps.

**Quick actions everywhere** - From any customer view, one-click to schedule service, start a deal, check trade value, etc.

**Smart defaults and context** - When creating a service appointment, pre-fill the customer’s preferred technician, usual service time, vehicle details.

## Role-Based Interface Adaptation

Different roles see different things first:

- **Sales staff** - leads, follow-ups, inventory matching customer needs
- **Service advisors** - today’s appointments, parts availability, technician schedules
- **Finance** - pending deals, payment processing, compliance checks
- **Management** - KPIs, staff performance, inventory turns
